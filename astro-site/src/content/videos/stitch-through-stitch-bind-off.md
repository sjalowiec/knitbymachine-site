This video demonstrates the stitch-through-stitch bind off, a hand-worked technique for finishing your knitting with a neat, flexible edge.

**Getting ready:**

When you're ready to bind off, pull all your needles forward to the holding position. Remove the yarn from the carriage—you'll be working by hand from here.

Cut the yarn, leaving a tail about three times the width of your knitting.

**The technique:**

Thread your tail onto a tapestry needle. Starting from the right side, insert the tapestry needle through the first stitch as if to purl, then through the second stitch as if to knit.

Slip the first stitch off the machine needle. Now insert the tapestry needle through the stitch you just removed (as if to knit) and through the next stitch on the machine (as if to purl).

Continue this pattern: each stitch gets worked twice before coming off the needle.

**Stitch size matters:**

The size of your bind-off stitches affects the finished edge. If you pull too tight, the edge will pucker. If too loose, it will be wavy.

Aim for stitches that match the size of your knitted fabric. Practice on a swatch first to get the feel.

**Finishing:**

When you reach the last stitch, pull the yarn through and weave in your end on the wrong side. Your bind-off is complete!

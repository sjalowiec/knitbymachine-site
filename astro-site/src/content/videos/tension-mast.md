<h2>Threading Up the Machine</h2>

<h3>Yarn Choice and Preparation</h3>

<p>It's time to thread up the machine and get knitting.</p>

<p>Before we get started, be sure you're using a yarn that's the appropriate weight for your machine.</p>

<p>We'll explore this in another video, but for now, just be sure you're using a medium-weight yarn that fits in the hooks of the needles.</p>

<h3>Do You Need Yarn Cones?</h3>

<p>Do you need yarn on cones for machine knitting? No.</p>

<p>All you need is to have the yarn wound in a way that it will easily feed into the machine.</p>

<p>Often, you can use a center pull ball from a yarn manufacturer.</p>

<p>Some knitters will use a ball winder and rewind everything. Skeins, hanks, balls, everything.</p>

<p>If your ball winder creates a flat bottom ball, you can also use the yarn from the outside of the ball.</p>

<p>Do whatever works for you.</p>

<p>You don't want to spend a whole lot of time dealing with knots as you're knitting.</p>

<h3>Starting to Thread the Mast</h3>

<p>Let's thread up the machine.</p>

<p>Working on the right side of the mast, take the end of the working yarn and thread it through the loop in the triangular yarn guide.</p>

<p>You'll then pass it through the mast tension dial.</p>

<h3>Setting the Mast Tension Dial</h3>

<p>Be sure to place the yarn between the disks.</p>

<p>Notice that there's a post inside. Be sure that the yarn passes under that post.</p>

<p>Adjust the tension dial to the middle setting, aligning the two triangles for now.</p>

<p>As you work with different yarns, you can adjust the flow of the yarn here based on the thickness of the yarn.</p>

<p>This tension dial, along with the carriage tension dial, impacts the gauge of your knitting.</p>

<h3>Feeder, Take-Up Spring, and Clip</h3>

<p>Place the yarn in the front loop of the front arm feeder.</p>

<p>Reach up and grab the right take-up spring and pass the yarn through the loop.</p>

<p>Secure the yarn in the clip on the mast to temporarily hold it.</p>

<p>This is how your mast should look.</p>

## Threading Up the Machine

### Yarn Choice and Preparation

It's time to thread up the machine and get knitting.

Before we get started, be sure you're using a yarn that's the appropriate weight for your machine.

We'll explore this in another video, but for now, just be sure you're using a medium-weight yarn that fits in the hooks of the needles.

### Do You Need Yarn Cones?

Do you need yarn on cones for machine knitting? No.

All you need is to have the yarn wound in a way that it will easily feed into the machine.

Often, you can use a center pull ball from a yarn manufacturer.

Some knitters will use a ball winder and rewind everything. Skeins, hanks, balls, everything.

If your ball winder creates a flat bottom ball, you can also use the yarn from the outside of the ball.

Do whatever works for you.

You don't want to spend a whole lot of time dealing with knots as you're knitting.

### Starting to Thread the Mast

Let's thread up the machine.

Working on the right side of the mast, take the end of the working yarn and thread it through the loop in the triangular yarn guide.

You'll then pass it through the mast tension dial.

### Setting the Mast Tension Dial

Be sure to place the yarn between the disks.

Notice that there's a post inside. Be sure that the yarn passes under that post.

Adjust the tension dial to the middle setting, aligning the two triangles for now.

As you work with different yarns, you can adjust the flow of the yarn here based on the thickness of the yarn.

This tension dial, along with the carriage tension dial, impacts the gauge of your knitting.

### Feeder, Take-Up Spring, and Clip

Place the yarn in the front loop of the front arm feeder.

Reach up and grab the right take-up spring and pass the yarn through the loop.

Secure the yarn in the clip on the mast to temporarily hold it.

This is how your mast should look.
